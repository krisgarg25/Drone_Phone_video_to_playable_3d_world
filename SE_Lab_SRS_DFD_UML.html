1. Software Requirements Specification

1.1 Introduction

Purpose. This SRS defines what the WALKABLE system must do, to what standard, and how each requirement is verified. It is written from the IEEE 830 / ISO 7301 outline and is the contract the DFDs (§2), class diagram (§3) and UML model (§4) are traced back to (§5).

Scope. The product accepts one or more video clips — a drone orbit or a walked phone scan — optionally with an AR pose log and flight telemetry, and produces (a) a camera-registered reconstruction, (b) a photorealistic 3D Gaussian splat, (c) a metric oriented frame, (d) a collision surface a character can stand on, and (e) survey deliverables in the formats the client brief names, opened in a browser-based viewer. It is a single-workstation, offline-capable tool: no cloud, no per-scene hand-tuning.

Product perspective. A pipelined, batch-sequential system of 17–18 dependency-ordered stages. Each stage is an independently invocable module writing typed artefacts under work/<scene>/; a code-hash completion marker makes every run resumable and re-runnable. Classical photogrammetry (COLMAP 4.1.1) supplies geometry, a neural renderer (gsplat 1.5.3 on PyTorch 2.4.1) supplies appearance, and everything downstream of the splat is deterministic computational geometry (voxelisation, rasterised heightfields, A*). Design rule: the neural stage is permitted to be only an image-synthesis stage — it is never trusted with geometry, with “down”, or with the metre.

Definitions and acronyms. SfM — Structure from Motion · MVS — Multi-View Stereo · GNSS / RTK — satellite positioning (centimetre-corrected) · GSD — ground sample distance · LoS — line of sight · SRS — software requirements specification · DoF — degrees of freedom · held-out checkpoint — a camera pose deliberately excluded from training and used only to measure accuracy · gate — check_world.py, the eleven-assertion build barrier.

1.2 Overall description

Real-world context. Mapping a site today means either slow, licensed photogrammetry that produces a model you can only look at, or a research demo that produces beautiful novel views with no surface, no scale, and no way in. The brief demands ≤ 1 m spatial accuracy and < 15 min processing for a 10-minute video, in OBJ / PLY / LAS / GeoTIFF / GLB / glTF / FBX. A Gaussian splat satisfies none of the three criteria that carry 70 % of the score by itself; the geometry and evidence layers built around it do. The system therefore treats provenance of every claimed number as a first-class requirement, not documentation.

User characteristics. Three personas, no assumed CV background: Surveyor (operates the drone or walks the phone; supplies footage and one physical anchor number), Analyst (configures presets/quality, reads reports, exports deliverables), Reviewer (judge or client — inspects the model, the measurements, and the evidence behind each claim, in a browser, without installing anything).

Typical operating scenarios. (1) Drone orbit — 12 s clip of a boulder field, run rocks --preset auto → registered, trained, gated, 65 m autopilot walk. (2) Phone room scan — capture console on the phone streams coverage feedback, 90 s walk with AR pose log → priors rescue the rotation-heavy segment that SfM alone drops. (3) Survey & measurement — RTK log aligns the reconstruction, ground control points are projected through the solved camera, and distances and heights come out in metres with their measurement basis attached.

Constraints. Windows 10/11 and NVIDIA CUDA 12.4 only (COLMAP and ffmpeg are vendored .exe) · must run end-to-end on a 6 GB laptop GPU (peak 1.26 GiB measured at the default high preset) · Python 3.12 for the pipeline and 3.10 for training, kept in separate virtual environments · zero compilation on a fresh clone · no per-scene magic constants — every threshold is derived from the scene or the GPU · all third-party code MIT / BSD / Apache, with the AGPL alternative surveyed and excluded from shipped paths · no network dependency at runtime.

Assumptions and dependencies. The operator supplies usable footage at ≥ 1080p with 60–70 % view overlap — the pipeline refuses sub-1080p input rather than silently degrading, and the capture console exists to prevent the failure upstream. At least one metric anchor exists per scene: GNSS, a surveyed control point, a cruise speed, or a camera height. A metre is named, not measured, when no GNSS is present, and the product must say so. COLMAP, gsplat and PlayCanvas are external artefacts pinned by the requirements in §3.1.

Apportioning of requirements. The viewer, the phone capture console and the combat/navigation layer are the same codebase deployed as an inspection tool for the survey outputs; requirements FR-01…FR-11 are core, FR-12…FR-15 are the survey and product layer, and FR-16…FR-19 are quality-of-evidence requirements that the client evaluation criteria impose.

1.6 Software product features and design constraints

Four features carry the product, and each is the subject of a diagram below: F1 Acquisition (FR-01, FR-02), F2 Reconstruction (FR-03…FR-07), F3 Walkable publication (FR-08…FR-11, FR-15), F4 Evidence and measurement (FR-12…FR-14, FR-16…FR-19). Three design constraints follow from measured incidents rather than preference: geometry may not come from the neural stage; scale must be a single named, auditable number; no claim ships without the measurement that produced it. A capsule of radius 0.34 m meeting an axis-aligned voxel wall has no lift term at all — which is a physics fact the architecture is obliged to respect, and the reason FR-08 measures two candidate grounds instead of tuning one.

Conclusion. The requirements above are not aspirational: every “H” row in Table 2 is implemented and exercised by ~550 assertions across 12 suites, and the two rows that fail today are reported as failing. The defining engineering result is that the intuitive fix was usually wrong and the log was usually right — a scene exported upside down because a RANSAC normal’s sign is arbitrary, a character standing on the sky because a raycast hit a canopy crust 20 m up, a crash attributed to landscape video that was really a zero-gaussian cloud, and three half-cell indexing errors that put the viewer’s ground 0.101 m from the physics surface. Each is now a named assertion, which is the point of specifying them here: a requirement you cannot measure is a claim, and a claim this system makes is required to carry the number that made it.
