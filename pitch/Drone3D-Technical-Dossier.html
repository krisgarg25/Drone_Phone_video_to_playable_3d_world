Radiance-field reconstruction can now draw a room from a phone clip at sixty frames a second in an ordinary browser. It still cannot tell you which way is down, how large the room is, or whether a person could cross it. This dossier sets out a method that recovers those three properties automatically, verifies the result by driving a body through it, and examines whether the approach is feasible, viable, and worth doing.

The question
The question being askedProblem statement

Given one uncontrolled video of a real place, can a system recover a physically consistent space without a human deciding anything?

Uncontrolled means filmed by an untrained person, on a phone or a cheap drone, with no markers, no depth sensor, no survey equipment and no knowledge of the room's true size. Without a human deciding anything means no per-scene settings, no manual ground selection and no operator who has learned which knobs to turn.

Physically consistent is the part worth defining carefully, because it is the part the literature does not deliver. It has four conditions.

Condition one
A known gravity vector

One axis is down, and the scene is oriented so that it is.

Condition two
A known metric scale

A metre is a metre, so distances mean something.

Condition three
A traversable surface

Something with an inside and an underside that stops a body.

Condition four
An independent check

The claim of walkability is measured, not asserted.

The object that satisfies conditions one to three. It is not a rendering of the hall; it is the surface a body would stand on, derived from the reconstruction.

Why this is a research question and not an engineering one

Conditions one and two are provably undecidable from images alone. Structure from motion recovers geometry only up to an unknown similarity transform, and a scene has no intrinsic up. Any solution must import information from outside the pixels. The interesting question is what weak, cheap outside signal is sufficient.

Conditions three and four are a different problem: appearance representations that interpolate well do not carry geometry, and geometry that carries no semantics of support cannot be checked for walkability. The interesting question there is what you can construct between the two without asking a human to label it.

Answer proposed here

Motion statistics and device pose logs for one and two. A voxelised support shell with the walker's own body dimensions as the constraint for three. A headless simulation of the actual viewer for four.

The similarity-transform ambiguity is the classical result of projective reconstruction; see [2] and [14] for the modern treatment.

Prior art · where the field arrivedAnd what each line of work left open
Appearance, then geometry, then speed
1
Radiance fields give photoreal appearance, slowly

NeRF [3] established that a continuous volumetric representation can be optimised from unposed images. Mip-NeRF 360 [4] extended it to unbounded, full-surround scenes, which is the regime casual video actually produces. Both are per-scene optimisations with no geometry output and no interactive frame rate.

2
Gaussian splatting makes it real time, and explicit

Kerbl et al. [1] replaced the implicit field with anisotropic Gaussians rasterised directly, reaching interactive rates and making the representation editable and inspectable. This is the representation used throughout this work. The two standard surveys [5][6] document the explosion of follow-up work and, notably, that almost all of it optimises image quality metrics.

3
Camera geometry is a solved commodity

Incremental structure from motion [2] gives poses and sparse structure from ordinary images, and remains the backbone of every serious pipeline. Recent work injects learned monocular surface priors to make it robust on sparse or planar scenes [14], which is the same failure mode casual video produces.

4
Surface extraction from splats is now active

SuGaR [7] and 2D Gaussian Splatting [8] constrain Gaussians to lie on a surface so a mesh can be pulled out. This is the closest prior art to condition three, and the difference is instructive: they optimise for a mesh that looks right. A mesh that stops a body has different requirements entirely, discussed on page 8.

Adjacent work that already needs what we are building
5
Robotics has started converting splats into traversability

Splatblox [9] builds a traversability-aware voxel map from Gaussian splats for outdoor navigation. This is strong evidence the need is real and current. It also assumes a robot with its own sensors on site, and it plans motion rather than certifying a reconstructed space for someone who is not there.

6
Embodied AI is short of real, walkable scenes

Habitat [10] and AI2-THOR [11] built the research platforms this field runs on, and both are hand-authored or artist-modelled environments. The bottleneck for embodied research has become scene supply, not scene rendering. Photographed real space is the obvious unexploited source.

7
Volumetric fusion is decades old and still the right primitive

Truncated signed distance fusion [12] and real-time fusion on mobile hardware [13] established that a voxel grid with a signed distance field gives you a surface with a consistent inside and outside. That property, not the rendering quality, is what a walker requires.

The consistent omission

Across all seven lines, the output is an artefact to be looked at. Gravity, scale and support are supplied by a human either during capture, during processing, or when the result is used. Nobody has made them properties of the reconstruction itself, because no benchmark scores them.

Our positioning

No new representation and no new learning. The contribution is a composition: recovering the missing physical properties from signals already present in casual footage, and making verification part of the pipeline rather than a human review step.

Why the four conditions are hardThe technical obstacles, individually
i
The up direction is a coin flip

A plane fitted to candidate floors has two normals of equal validity. A naive implementation picks one and is right half the time, which in this domain means exporting entire scenes upside down at a rate indistinguishable from success. The failure is silent because the render still looks plausible from most viewpoints.

What makes it tractable: cameras are above the ground they film, essentially always. That asymmetry is a statistical signal, and it survives even in footage with no visible floor in most frames.

ii
Monocular reconstruction has no scale

This is not an implementation weakness, it is a property of the geometry. Images are invariant under scaling the world and the camera rig together. Any pipeline that reports metres has imported that information from somewhere, and most pipelines quietly import it from a human.

What makes it tractable: two independent, cheap, physically grounded anchors exist in ordinary footage: typical handheld camera height, and typical drone cruise speed. Device pose logs supply a third and best one directly.

iii
An appearance representation has no surface

Gaussians are a distribution, not a boundary. They have opacity, not solidity. Extracting a mesh by fitting to them produces a surface that approximates what the viewer sees, which is not the same as a surface that blocks a body: it can be watertight and still let a walker through a table, or it can be thick enough to stand on and still float above the actual floor.

What makes it tractable: stop asking the surface to be faithful to the image. Ask it to be faithful to support, and make the walker's own dimensions the definition of a step.

iv
Every quality metric is a picture metric

Reconstruction error against held-out views is the field's standard measure and it is genuinely informative about appearance. It says nothing about whether the scene is usable. A world can score well and be unnavigable, and the pipeline will report success with a straight face.

What makes it tractable: the property you care about is behavioural. Simulate it. The measurement is then cheap, automatic, and capable of contradicting the system's own optimistic summary.

Obstacle iv is the one that changes how the other three are engineered: a measurement that can say no forces the rest of the system to be honest.

Solution approach · the method in one viewFive ideas, in the order they are applied

Recover the physics from what the footage already knows, then believe nothing you have not simulated.

Idea one
Read the
footage before
processing it

Motion profile, blur distribution and rotation dominance are measured before any solver runs. They select a capture regime and set solver parameters. The system diagnoses rather than defaults, so a walked living room is never routed through an aerial pipeline.

Addresses the settings problem at its root: the settings are an output.

Idea two
Import scale
from physics,
not from a human

Camera height and camera speed are physical quantities the footage contains. Either fixes the metric scale of the reconstruction. Where a device supplies its own pose log, that supersedes both and becomes a prior inside the solver rather than a post-hoc multiplier.

Conditions one and two, resolved from signals already present.

Idea three
Build support,
not a mesh

A voxelised shell derived from the cloud, in which any change in height becomes a vertical face. The walker's own capsule dimensions decide what counts as climbable, so traversability is a property of a body rather than an opinion about a surface.

Condition three, deliberately not solved by mesh extraction.

Idea four
Choose by
measurement,
not by rule

Where two plausible answers exist, build both and test them. Two candidate floors are constructed, routed, and raced; the one the walker travels further on is the one that ships. Where a budget is needed, it is read from the machine at run time rather than configured.

Removes the judgement calls that make a pipeline bespoke.

Idea five
Verify by
simulation, and
allow a no

A rule set grades the finished world, then a character is driven through it unattended with its trajectory logged. The reported outcome is cross-checked against the trajectory, because a summary statistic can be arithmetically correct and still describe something that did not happen.

Condition four, and the mechanism that keeps the other four honest.

The method is classical in its parts and unusual in its composition: none of the five ideas requires a learned model, which is what makes the whole thing inspectable and cheap to run.

Approach detail · camera geometry from unusable footageIdea one and part of idea two

Casual video is the worst possible input for structure from motion, and it is the only input people have.

Handheld footage is blurry, has motion blur concentrated in the frames that turn rather than translate, repeats the same wall at slightly different times, and often contains long stretches with almost no parallax. A solver fed that directly either fails or registers a fraction of the frames and silently produces a warped model.

Four interventions
Select frames by information, not by interval. Frames are binned across the clip's timeline so coverage is even, then a sharpness floor is derived from the footage's own blur distribution rather than fixed, and near duplicates are rejected. This removes exactly the turning-in-place frames that most damage a solve.
Supply the true focal length. A solver's default guess of 1.2 times sensor width can be wrong by roughly 45% on a phone lens, and a wrong focal length corrupts depth in proportion to distance. The capture surface exports a real calibration alongside the video, and the pipeline consumes it automatically when it is present.
Inject device poses as priors. Where a phone records its own trajectory, those positions enter the reconstruction as metric priors. This is the difference between inferring scale after the fact and having scale constrain the solve throughout, and it is measurably the strongest single input in the system.
Refuse to fail on the first attempt. The solver binary is probed for which options it actually supports, so an unsupported flag is dropped rather than fatal. A failing solve then descends a ladder of alternatives: a different feature matcher, a different mapper, relaxed thresholds. Known-bad legacy assets are classified as unsupported input rather than surfacing as a crash.

A frame from an ordinary consumer drone clip. Nothing on the ground, no markers, no survey control. This is the input class, not a curated one.

Why this is the right place to spend effort

Everything downstream inherits the camera solution. A solve that registers two thirds of frames does not produce a two-thirds-quality world; it produces holes in the floor, a warped perimeter, and a route that stops for no visible reason. Robustness here is worth more than fidelity later.

Relation to the literature

The approach is deliberately non-learning. Recent work reaches the same robustness by fusing learned monocular surface priors into the solver [14], which is more powerful and also harder to explain, harder to audit, and dependent on a model whose failure modes are not visible in the output. Blur-aware selection, real calibration and device priors get most of the way there with nothing to trust but arithmetic.

A learned prior remains the obvious upgrade path and is on the research agenda on page 17.

Multi-clip fusion

Separate clips of the same place are matched into one space rather than reconstructed separately and aligned afterwards, because alignment after the fact inherits each clip's own scale error. This is the least robust capability in the system and is treated as an open problem, not a finished one.

Approach detail · establishing a frameGravity and metric scale

Two physical facts, weak on their own, sufficient together.

The reconstruction is a similarity class, not a metric object. To collapse that class to a single world you need one number. Three candidate sources exist, and the method uses whichever the footage supplies, in a fixed order of preference.

a
Device-recorded positions

Strongest. A phone that logged its own trajectory in metres gives both scale and up directly, and gives them before the solve rather than after. Available on any modern handset with the capture surface, and on either major mobile augmented-reality stack.

b
Handheld camera height

Weak but almost always available. A person walking and filming holds a phone at a fairly stable height above the floor they are filming. One declared constant converts the camera solution's own statistics into a metric scale, and simultaneously resolves the up direction because the cameras must sit above the surface they see.

c
Aerial cruise speed

The aerial counterpart. A consumer drone holding position in light wind and flying a steady orbit moves at a characteristic speed, which fixes scale from the camera path's own geometry.

The honest caveat

These are priors, not measurements. A phone held at the waist, a drone in a gust, or a clip filmed from a vehicle all shift the answer. The method therefore reports which anchor it used and what it assumed, so the scale is a stated input rather than a hidden constant. For survey-accurate work the device log or a known dimension in frame is required.

How up is decided

Candidate orientations are scored against a single asymmetry: the cameras are above the ground they film. The approach that puts the majority of camera height above the fitted surface wins. This is robust precisely because it does not require identifying the floor as an object; it only requires that whatever the ground is, it is below the lenses.

What the frame then unlocks
Distances
metres, not pixels
Clearances

Slopes

Without a frame, none of these are well-formed questions. This is why framing is placed before the surface stage rather than as a display-time transform.

A consequence that shaped the whole codebase

Because scale is inferred per scene, every threshold downstream must be expressed relative to it. Any check written in bare metres will pass in a large world and fail in a small one for reasons that look like randomness. The system therefore forbids absolute distances and absolute element counts after this stage, and that single constraint removed most of its historical instability.

Failure mode we hit

An earlier version fitted a plane and took its normal. Half the time the sign was wrong and the scene exported inverted. A walker's ground check then searched a band that began below the floor of a small room, so grounding never registered and a zero-fall result was reported for a character that was airborne throughout. Both are documented rather than removed from the record.

Classical treatment of the ambiguity in [2]; the modern learned alternative in [14]. Our approach is deliberately the non-learned one.

Approach detail · from appearance to supportThe central technical choice

We did not extract a mesh. That was the decision.

The obvious route is SuGaR [7] or 2DGS [8]: constrain the Gaussians to a surface, pull out a triangle mesh, hand it to a physics engine. It produces a beautiful object and a bad floor. A visually faithful mesh is watertight in the wrong places and paper-thin in the right ones; a table top is a surface, but the space under the table is not a hole in the world.

What a walker needs is not a boundary. It is a support field: for every column of the world, what height will hold a body up.

The construction

The cloud is voxelised. Within that grid, occupied cells become solid and the boundary between occupied and empty becomes a face. Crucially, every change in height across a cell becomes a vertical wall rather than a ramp, so the resulting surface cannot be walked up at an angle that the underlying geometry does not actually support.

Traversability is then decided by the body, not by the surface. A capsule of a stated width and height cannot ascend a riser taller than its own step allowance. A 0.34 m capsule cannot climb a 0.35 m step at all. That converts an unanswerable question, is this floor walkable, into a routable one, is there a path for this body.

Two candidate grounds, raced

A clipped shell and a measured height field disagree about where the floor is, and neither reliably wins. Rather than pick by heuristic, both are built, both are routed, and the walker is sent across each. The one that supports a longer traverse ships. The race costs under a second and it removed a class of decisions that previously required a human.

Across the corpus the winner inverts between scenes, and no local roughness statistic predicts it. That negative result is the whole justification for measuring instead of choosing.

Reconstruction and support field together. The mesh is not an approximation of the photograph; it is a separate object answering a different question.

Why this is not just a collision proxy

The same field answers the questions an actual user asks: where can someone stand, how far is the nearest way out, what blocks sight of the thing being inspected, what is the vertical drop at this edge. Those become measurements over a structure with a defined inside and outside, rather than judgements made by whoever is looking at the scan.

The consistency constraint

Three derived objects must agree or the product is a lie: the physics surface, the planned route, and the painted ground the browser shows beneath the splats. A rule measures the median separation between them and refuses the world if they have drifted. Without this, a viewer can walk convincingly on a floor that is visibly somewhere else.

Trade-off accepted

The support field is coarse. It will not satisfy a drafting office and it is not the deliverable a construction workflow expects. A clean mesh out of the same evidence is listed as required future work, not as something we already have.

The signed-distance intuition is long established [12][13]; the novelty is applying it to a radiance-field cloud with the walker's body as the traversability definition.

Approach detail · removing the operatorMaking the pipeline unattended

Every number the pipeline needs is read from the scene or from the machine. Nothing is configured.

This sounds like an operational nicety and it is actually a technical requirement, because a per-scene setting is a hidden human in the loop. It also makes the system's behaviour depend on the hardware it happens to run on, which has to be handled explicitly rather than wished away.

The three budgets, in dependency order
1
Image detail

Decided first, from memory free at the moment the run starts. Getting the order wrong is the classic cause of an opaque failure, because the element cap is then set for a memory profile the frames do not fit inside.

2
Model capacity

Follows from what is left after rendering cost. A fixed overhead, a cost per megapixel of render and a cost per million elements, with a safety fraction. The coefficients were fitted against this system's own crashes on one reference card and are honest about that.

3
Surface resolution

Coarsened until the scene's own footprint fits the memory ceiling of the mesh builder. A hard tool limit, discovered from a real crash, encoded as a budget instead of a failure.

The rule that enforces it

A new fixed constant must be argued for in writing, with a reason it cannot be measured. This is a code-review constraint and it is the single most effective reliability mechanism in the project.

The consequence, and how it is contained

Because capacity follows free memory, the same clip legitimately produces different-sized models on different days. That is not a bug to be eliminated; it is the price of running on hardware nobody has reserved. It has one hard implication:

nothing downstream may count anything.

No absolute element count, no absolute cell count, no absolute distance. Every check is a share of the cloud, a spread across the camera path, or a fraction of the scene's own footprint. An absolute threshold on a machine-dependent quantity is a nondeterministic test, and nondeterministic tests are what produced this project's earlier reputation for random failure.

Degradation is designed, not improvised

Thin evidence. Where geometry is too sparse to bound a room confidently, the region is bounded by the flight path and the ground below it, and the world ships with a stated warning rather than a crash.

Killed mid-training. Training checkpoints continuously, so an operating-system kill that no exception handler can observe still leaves a rescuable model.

Evidence that cannot be produced. Comparison renders are advisory. A locked output file must not destroy an otherwise valid world.

Anything that would be a traceback. Failures are classified into a fixed taxonomy and each class maps to a repair, which is recorded in the run report so the repair is auditable after the fact.

Incrementality is part of the same argument: each stage's completion marker hashes the source of the script that ran, so editing a stage invalidates exactly it and everything downstream. A cached pass cannot conceal an unfixed bug.

This is the least publishable part of the work and the most load-bearing. It is also the reason the system can be operated by someone who did not build it.

Approach detail · verification as a pipeline stageThe part that can say no

A reconstruction is a claim. This is the part of the system that tries to falsify it.

Two mechanisms, deliberately different in kind.

Mechanism one · a rule set over the finished world

Thirteen checks at two severities. The strict ones are conditions for shipping: does measured ground exist at all, is the terrain a floor rather than a ceiling, does the walker begin on something that supports it, does the collision shell actually exist, is it the same surface the route was planned across, is there a route long enough to be worth testing. The quality ones grade coverage, camera placement, flatness and drift.

Strict failures are never relaxed to produce a passing run. In the current corpus two of ten scenes are refused certification by their own gate, including the scene used in the public demonstration.

Mechanism two · an unattended traverse

The real viewer is launched headless, the real physics engine runs, and a character is driven along the generated route with position, grounding and falls logged at regular intervals. The demonstration and the verification share one code path, so a demo cannot be fixed independently of a test.

The cross-check that matters

Distance travelled is recomputed from the logged trajectory and compared with the distance the run reports. If they disagree, the harness says so. This exists because a summary statistic can be internally consistent and describe something that never happened.

Mid-traverse inside a rebuilt field with the support surface visible. The on-screen telemetry is the harness's own output, not a caption.

What this has actually caught

A walker reported airborne across every sample of a run while its summary claimed a clean traverse with zero falls. Nothing that asserted correctness could have found this; only a second, independent measurement of the same event did.

Distance accumulating without travel: a character pressed against an obstacle, reporting metres it never crossed. The sampled route covered a fraction of the claimed distance and the discrepancy surfaced immediately.

A ground check searching a band that began below the floor of a small room, which made an entire class of results vacuous. Root cause was the scale-inference issue from page 7 propagating into a threshold written in absolute metres.

An unparseable value in a generated document that killed the walk test for a scene everything else considered healthy. Every browser-facing artefact is now checked for parseability as a condition of shipping.

Relation to prior art

Traversability estimation from radiance fields is established in robotics [9][15]. What is uncommon here is making it a gate on the reconstruction rather than a planner on the robot: the scene is certified before anyone is asked to trust it, and the certification can fail the pipeline's own output.

Methodologically this is the strongest claim in the dossier, because it is the only one that is falsifiable without human judgement.

What is actually new hereStated narrowly, on purpose

No new representation. No new model. One new claim.

Every ingredient is published and well understood. The claim is that combining them in a specific order produces an artefact class that does not currently exist: a reconstruction that arrives with a stated frame, a derived support surface, and an independent certificate that a body can traverse it.

Four things we would defend as contributions

Frame recovery as an automatic stage. Gravity and metric scale resolved from motion statistics and device logs, applied before geometry is used rather than at display time.

Support fields over radiance clouds. A voxelised surface whose purpose is to stop a body, with the body's dimensions defining traversability instead of a slope threshold.

Measurement instead of configuration. Budgets derived from live machine state, all downstream thresholds expressed relatively, and a review rule that a constant must justify why it cannot be measured.

Verification as a pipeline stage that can block a release. A rule set plus an unattended traverse, with a cross-check capable of contradicting the system's own summary.

Four things we would not defend

Rendering quality. Reconstruction error against held-out views is below what published work reports for this representation. We are not competitive on appearance.

The camera solver. It is a mature external tool, used close to its defaults with a retry ladder around it.

Learning anything. There is no trained component. Every decision is arithmetic over measured quantities, which is a design position, not an oversight.

Scale of evidence. The corpus is small, self-captured, and produced on one machine. Nothing here has survived contact with other people's footage.

Where the intellectual risk sits

The composition is defensible; the assumptions behind it are not exotic. Two of them could be overtaken. Generative reconstruction that produces geometry with a consistent frame directly would make our framing stage unnecessary. Standardised splat-to-mesh pipelines that carry semantics of support would make the support field a commodity. Both are plausible within the horizon and neither is solved today.

The durable part is the verification layer. Whatever produces the geometry, someone still has to establish that a person can stand in it, and that will not be settled by an image quality score.

Positioning against the seven prior-art lines on page 3, stated as composition rather than invention.

How to read the strength column

Strong means the artefacts exist on disk, the run is reproducible from the repository, and the claim does not depend on a judgement call.

The one that matters most

Indoor life-size walking is the claim a customer would assume is included in the phrase walkable world. It is not. The captured footprint of a small room supports only a couple of square metres of genuine floor, and the current answer is to shrink the observer. That is an honest limitation and the first item on the research agenda.

Corpus, stated plainly

Ten reconstructed scenes, seven distinct captures, all filmed by the author, all processed on one machine, over roughly three weeks. That is the entire empirical basis of this dossier and it is thin.

Feasibility conclusion: the method works on the input class it was designed for. It has not been tested on the input class it will be sold to.

Feasibility · the technical risksWhat could stop this working, and what answers it
Risk one
Scale inference is a prior, not a measurement

A phone held low, a drone in wind, or a clip shot from a vehicle shifts the inferred metre. Everything downstream inherits the error proportionally, and nothing in the output looks wrong because of it.

Answer. Prefer device pose logs, which give scale directly. Where absent, report the anchor used and its assumption, and expose a single known dimension in frame as a correction. For anything requiring survey accuracy, treat inferred scale as a placeholder and say so on the artefact.

Risk two
The support field is coarse by construction

A voxel shell that reliably stops a body will not satisfy anyone expecting a clean surface model, and the cell size is capped by the mesh builder's memory ceiling on large scenes.

Answer. Separate the two deliverables. Support fields answer behavioural questions; surfaces answer geometric ones. Surface extraction from the same evidence is future work with a known route through published methods [7][8], not something to fake with a coarse proxy.

Risk three
The camera solver is not ours

The most failure-prone stage depends on a mature external binary whose priorities we do not control, and the training stage is pinned to one interpreter, framework and compute combination.

Answer. The retry ladder and option probing already absorb most drift, and the health check turns an environment mismatch into a named, fixable condition. A learned alternative exists and is credible [14]; adopting one is a swap behind a stable interface rather than a rewrite.

Risk four
Verification can only check what it was told to check

Thirteen rules encode our current understanding of what a usable world is. Anything outside that list passes silently, which is the exact failure mode we are trying to eliminate.

Answer. The rules are data, not code, and each records the measurement behind its verdict, so a new defect class becomes a new rule rather than a redesign. The behavioural traverse is the backstop precisely because it does not need to know what is wrong to notice that movement failed.

Risk five
Thin and self-selected evidence

One operator, one machine, one capture style, three weeks. Failure rates quoted here are about this corpus, not about the world's footage.

Answer. There is no substitute. The first external batch is the experiment that matters and nothing in this dossier should be read as pre-empting it.

Risk six
Delivery weight and platform lock

A room currently ships as an uncompressed point file over a hundred megabytes, and the toolchain is vendored for one operating system and one graphics vendor.

Answer. Both are known and both have established routes: compressed interchange formats are already in use across the ecosystem [16][17], and portability is unglamorous engineering rather than research. Neither is a reason the approach could not work; both are reasons it cannot yet be used widely.

Feasibility verdict: no identified risk threatens the core method. Risks one, two and five threaten the value of its output; three, four and six threaten its reach.

Viability · can this sustain itselfCost, trajectory, dependence, path to scale

The expensive part of this gets cheaper every year without us touching it.

The work that is specific to us, namely framing, support and verification, is a rounding error against the two stages the entire field competes on. That asymmetry is the viability argument, and it is favourable: our cost base falls with commodity hardware while our contribution is engineering that does not.

Why that matters commercially

A cost base dominated by commodity compute means margin improves as hardware improves, and the two dominant stages are exactly where a large body of published optimisation work is already pushing. Nothing in our differentiator depends on owning scarce resources.

Four viability conditions
1
Does the technique have a future, or are we early to a dead end?

Radiance-field representation is now the default for interactive capture: hundreds of products, compression interchange formats in active use [16][17], browser-native rendering, and sustained publication volume [5][6]. This is a settled bet, not a speculative one.

2
Is there demand for a walkable output specifically?

The clearest signal is adjacent: robotics now converts splats into traversability maps [9][15], and embodied simulation platforms are starved of real environments [10][11]. Both need exactly the artefact this method produces. Insurance, safety inspection, heritage recording and remote training are downstream of the same property.

3
Can it be operated by someone who did not build it?

Partly. Zero per-scene configuration and a self-diagnosing health check are designed for this. It has not been tested on other people's footage, and that is the open question, not this page.

4
Is the licence position clean?

Yes. Everything first-party is permissive, and every heavy dependency is BSD, Apache or MIT. No copyleft component sits in the pipeline, so a hosted service built on this carries no obligation to disclose its own source.

What would make this not viable

A generative model that emits metric, watertight, walkable scenes directly from video would collapse the method's advantage to the verification layer alone. That is the scenario worth watching, and it is why the durable asset is the certificate rather than the geometry.

Viability verdict: viable as a technique with a narrow but real window. Not yet viable as a product, because the hosting layer, the delivery weight and the external corpus all have to exist first.

Impact · what changes if this worksConsequences of the four conditions being automatic

The value is not a nicer scan. It is that a reconstruction becomes something you can act inside rather than look at.

Safety and inspection
A site you can walk
before you go to it

Support and scale turn a scan into a route. Where can a person stand, how far to the nearest way out, what is the drop at this edge, what blocks the view of the thing being checked. Those become measurements a supervisor can make from a desk, and a trainee can rehearse before their first visit.

Depends on conditions one, two, three and four. This is the application that needs verification most, because an uncertified walkthrough of a hazardous site is worse than none.

Insurance and claims
A room that can be
revisited after it
is cleared

A loss is filmed on a phone once, and the scene then has to support distances under challenge long after the room is rebuilt. A metric frame with a recorded traverse is what turns a photograph into evidence rather than illustration.

Depends heavily on scale being defensible, which is precisely risk one. Honest reporting of the anchor used is a requirement here, not a nicety.

Heritage and reconstruction
Walk a building
that no longer
exists

Fire-damaged, demolished or war-damaged structures survive as footage. Automatic framing makes that footage navigable at human height without a specialist rebuilding it, which changes who can use an archive at all.

The most forgiving application: scale error is tolerable and the verification step is what makes a reconstruction trustworthy enough to present publicly.

Embodied AI and simulation

Robotics and agent research run on hand-built environments because real reconstructions arrive without a floor, without scale and without a traversability guarantee. Supplying those three automatically makes photographed space usable as training data, which is currently the binding constraint on realistic simulation [10][11][9].

Accessibility of space

Route planning for a wheelchair, a stroller or low vision is a support-and-clearance problem, and it is the same computation the walker already performs. A reconstruction that knows where a body can go is a navigation source, not a picture.

Cheap documentation everywhere

The capture skill is already universal and the processing skill is what this removes. That is the difference between surveying being something you commission and being something you do while you are there.

Every one of these depends on the same four conditions being automatic. None depends on rendering quality, which is where the rest of the field is competing.

Benefits of the specific approach takenWhy non-learning, why verify, why derive
Three design choices and what each buys
1
Nothing learned, everything measured

Every decision is arithmetic over quantities that can be printed in a report. When a world is wrong, the reason is a number someone can look at. A learned component would probably be more accurate on hard footage and would make every failure opaque. For a system whose entire value is a certificate, auditability is worth more than accuracy.

2
Verification built in rather than added on

The output is a world plus a record that a body crossed it. That changes what the artefact can be used for: it can be relied on by someone who is not able to judge a reconstruction by eye, which is almost every actual consumer of a scan. It also gives the project a measurement that can embarrass it, which is the only reason to believe the rest of its numbers.

3
Budgets derived from live machine state

Runs on whatever hardware is present rather than on a specified rig, which is what makes consumer-grade and on-premises deployment realistic. The cost is nondeterministic model size, and that cost is paid down by forbidding absolute thresholds everywhere downstream.

Benefits that follow structurally

Runs where the data has to stay. No cloud dependency in the current design, so footage that cannot leave a site is not a blocker. This is an advantage in inspection, energy and defence contexts rather than a limitation.

Explainable to a regulator. A run report states what was assumed, what was measured, what failed and what was repaired. That is a requirement in exactly the sectors on the previous page, and it is hard to obtain from an end-to-end learned pipeline.

Improvable without retraining. A new defect class becomes a new rule and a new budget, not a new dataset and a new model. The system's failure taxonomy is the thing that grows.

Open by default. Permissive licensing throughout means the verification idea can be adopted by others rather than only competed with, which in a young field is plausibly the faster route to it becoming expected.

What we give up, stated honestly

Robustness on the hardest footage. A learned prior handles a textureless wall or a near-planar scene better than any amount of retry logic, and we know it because those are precisely the cases that currently fail here. The design position is that auditability wins for now and the prior can be added behind a stable interface later. That is a bet, and one worth revisiting when there is external evidence either way.

The single most valuable property

A reconstruction that can refuse to be called finished. A pipeline that is allowed to lose to its own checks is a cultural choice most products will not make.

These are design consequences rather than results, and are labelled as reasoning rather than measurement.

Methodological commitments carried forward

Four rules that the next phase of work inherits from this one.

§

Every capability ships with a measurement that can contradict it. If a claim cannot be checked by something other than a human looking, it is not finished.

§

No absolute thresholds after framing. This is what keeps machine-dependent behaviour from looking like random failure.

§

Degrade with a stated limit rather than discard. A partial world with a warning is worth more than a failed job.

§

Negative results are recorded. The ground-race finding that no roughness statistic predicts the winner is more useful than a heuristic that pretends otherwise.

Whether the verification layer is a product requirement or a research curiosity. Two years of external usage will answer that and nothing written here can.

Limitations, and what this must not be used forStated before anyone has to ask

A walkable world is a claim about safety. That constrains how it may be used.

The moment a reconstruction certifies that someone can cross a space, it is carrying an implication that they are safe to do so. That is a serious responsibility for an artefact generated by an automatic pipeline from a phone clip, and it should be bounded explicitly rather than discovered in an incident.

Not a substitute for a survey

Scale here is inferred from priors unless a device log or a known dimension supplies it. Nothing produced by this pipeline should support a structural decision, a legal boundary, an evacuation certification, or a compliance sign-off without independent measurement.

Not a statement about what is not visible

The support field describes what the cameras saw. A floor that was never filmed from above, a opening hidden behind furniture, or a surface that was wet on the day and dry now are all invisible to it, and the certificate does not and cannot speak to them.

The certificate must travel with the world

A scene stripped of its verdict file is a scene stripped of its limits. Any sharing surface has to carry the warnings, the anchor used, and the refusal status where one applies. Suppressing them for a cleaner presentation would be the most damaging possible misuse of this work.

Privacy, because the input is a home

The capture device films the inside of people's private spaces, at metric scale, with a route through them. That is a materially sensitive artefact, not a photograph. Consent at capture, retention limits, and the ability to delete a derived world along its evidence must be design requirements of any hosted version rather than policy added after an incident.

The claim, in one line

A place is only
reconstructed when
someone can stand in it.

Everything in this dossier is in service of that sentence, and everything that remains unproven is a consequence of it being a hard claim to make cheaply. The representation is settled, the geometry is commodity, and the missing piece was never a better picture. It was gravity, a metre, a floor, and the willingness to let a system fail its own test.

How to check any number here

Clone the repository, run the bootstrap, then the health check. The fast suites reproduce the 232 checks. The end-to-end matrix reproduces the per-stage timings and the gate verdicts on every take in the corpus. The run report, the verdict file and the walk log are written to disk by each job and are the primary source for pages 12 to 14.

What to look at first

The two scenes the gate refused. They are the fastest way to judge whether this system measures itself honestly, which is the property everything else here depends on.
